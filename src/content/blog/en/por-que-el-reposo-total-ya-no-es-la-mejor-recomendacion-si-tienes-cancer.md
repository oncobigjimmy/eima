For years, the standard advice was clear: “don’t exert yourself, rest”. However, medicine has evolved and we now know that, in most situations, staying in bed as a matter of course **can be counterproductive**.

The science is clear that adapted physical exercise is not only safe, but also a key part of helping the body cope better with the process.

The main international oncology societies no longer see exercise as an optional “extra”, but as a therapeutic tool to help the body withstand **the toxicity of treatments**, whether chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy or hormone therapies.

## Exercise as a shield and medicine

This is not about training for a marathon, but about using appropriately dosed movement to protect your body on three critical fronts:

- **Overcoming cancer-related fatigue:** it reduces the extreme tiredness that often accompanies the process and which, surprisingly, does not improve simply by sleeping more.
- **Physical protection:** it helps preserve muscle mass and bone density, which are often weakened by medication, maintaining your independence.
- **Emotional wellbeing:** it improves mood and perceived health at times of constant uncertainty, reducing the feeling of isolation.

## How to start without fear

Starting does not mean improvising. For exercise to truly act as medicine, an initial assessment must take into account the type of cancer, your stage of treatment, your current symptoms and your personal history of movement.
